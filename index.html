Police & Thief

A hidden-movement chase through a building of connected rooms.

How to play

  1. The thief (Player A) picks a starting room while the police (Player B) look away. It can't be the entrance, an exit or a vault.
  2. The police start at the entrance. Each turn they move through a door to a neighbouring room, or wait where they are. Walls (thick grey lines) block the way.
  3. After each police turn, the thief moves to a neighbouring room it has never visited. The thief cannot wait.
  4. Heist: both players can see the exits and the three vaults (◆). Walking into a vault takes its loot. With 2 loot, walking into either exit wins. Robbing a vault trips a silent alarm. On Easy and Normal the police hear it at the start of their next turn. On Hard it reaches them one turn late, after the thief has moved on.
  5. The police win by entering the thief's room, or if the thief walks into them or is cornered. In Heist they also win if time runs out. In Classic, the thief wins by staying free until time runs out.

Signals

The police's room lights up at the start of every police turn. The strongest signal wins.

  • Hot. The thief is in a room next to you right now.
  • Warm. The thief was in your room or next to it one move ago.
  • Faint. The thief passed through your room or next to it a few moves ago: 2–4 on Easy, 2–3 on Normal. Hard has no Faint signal.
  • Cold. No sign of the thief nearby.

The thief gets one warning: Footsteps when the police are in a room next door.

Design notes

Why the police can wait. On a square grid, every move changes the distance between the two players by one. Without a wait move, half of all hiding spots could never be caught. Waiting lets the police fix that.

Why the police can see the vaults. Known vaults give the police something to plan around: guard the loot, or guard the exits. The thief has to step into a known room to win, and the silent alarm makes each grab a risk.

Why blue to red. The colours follow a heat scale, so red means hottest and blue means cold. Each signal also has a name, so it still reads without colour.
